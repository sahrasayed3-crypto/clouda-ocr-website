Why this exists

Humans can read them. Machines often cannot use them reliably.

Arabic scanned documents, archival material, and degraded pages carry enormous knowledge. OCR errors, broken reading order, and lost document structure make that knowledge hard to search, structure, and use in modern AI systems. Clouda OCR exists to close that gap with evidence, not claims.

What Clouda OCR is

One project, one name, one narrow wedge.

Clouda OCR is an Arabic-first, open-source document-processing and OCR infrastructure project for turning PDFs and scanned documents into reliable, editable, machine-usable outputs. The current runtime handles trusted born-digital text and document routing; scanned-page OCR remains gated behind validated model integration while model evaluation and data preparation continue.

The focus is deliberately narrow: Arabic-first OCR and document conversion for difficult and archival documents, with reliable editable and searchable structured output and independently measured model performance. Search, RAG, and archive intelligence are downstream future use cases — not current product claims.

01

Who it is for

Arabic-heavy archives, digitization teams, researchers, publishers, and document-processing teams that need editable, searchable output from difficult PDFs and scans while keeping control over their data and infrastructure.

02

What it is not

Not a trained-model company. Not a SaaS platform. Not a claim that scanned-page OCR is already solved — every model claim must first survive independent Arabic evaluation.

03

How it decides

Model choice follows evidence. If existing open models solve the problem, Clouda OCR uses them; only a demonstrated, persistent gap justifies adaptation or training.

What exists today

What actually works — stated plainly.

No mockups, no fabricated output. The released runtime processes trusted born-digital PDFs today, and every page it cannot honestly process is routed to an explicit review state instead of being passed off as OCR.

Why Clouda OCR

Why Clouda OCR instead of a single OCR model?

Strong OCR models already exist — and Clouda OCR is built to use them, not to compete with them blindly. The project is model-agnostic: it independently evaluates models on Arabic documents and keeps the strongest suitable open, self-hostable option rather than binding the system to one vendor or one checkpoint.

Independent evaluationA published Arabic benchmark measures candidate models before anything is chosen.
Document routing & trust gatesPages are classified and routed honestly — including pending_ocr_model and review_required states.
RTL-aware reconstructionArabic paragraph direction, page order, and boundaries are preserved in editable output.
Provenance-aware data preparationLicensed, seed-controlled generation and preparation keep the data pipeline auditable.
Benchmark infrastructureFrozen manifests, stored outputs, and reproducible scoring make results comparable over time.
Integration/adaptation pathsIf a persistent gap is proven, adapting or training a model becomes an engineering decision — not a hope.
The goal is not to train a model merely to own a model. The goal is reliable Arabic document processing. If existing models solve the problem, Clouda OCR uses them. If evidence shows a persistent gap, adaptation and training become justified.

Current evaluation

A larger benchmark is running — results come later.

An evaluation on 462 held-out Arabic pages, defined by a frozen manifest, across a 10-model evaluation plan is in progress and currently paused pending additional compute capacity. Results will be published only after the evaluation is complete and validated.

Results: not published yet. No partial leaderboard, no interim winner, and no unvalidated metric claims will be shown here until the full run is finished and its aggregates are validated. Published v0.1.0 results remain separate.
Why now

The pieces exist. The next decision is compute-blocked.

Benchmark v0.1.0 is published. The open-source runtime is released (v0.2.0). The expanded 462-page evaluation is frozen and ready. What blocks the next technical decision is compute and infrastructure capacity — not the absence of a plan. And the project is deliberately refusing to train a model before the evidence justifies it.
